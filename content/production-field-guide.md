# 17. Four team constellations

Type is not a hiring rubric, a diagnosis, or a way to predict talent. A graphics programmer can be any type. The practical use of a type pattern is to anticipate what an otherwise capable person may over-prioritize under pressure, and which counter-perspective can stabilize the work.

<section class="type-card">

## Analysts and systems builders — NT field

An NT-heavy field often produces architectural ambition, conceptual rigor, rapid reframing, and confidence in system-level intervention. Its strength is making hidden structure discussable. Its risk is allowing the model to become more real than the users, craft constraints, or social adoption path.

The field benefits from explicit contact points: target-hardware captures, migration history, artistic invariants, and feedback from people who must operate the system. Ask the group to distinguish elegance, leverage, and actual value. The goal is not to weaken abstraction; it is to make abstraction answerable to a scene.

</section>

<section class="type-card">

## Counselors and culture shapers — NF field

An NF-heavy field organizes around meaning, possibility, identity, and the effect of work on people. Its strength is protecting purpose while crossing disciplinary boundaries. Its risk is that a compelling mission or interpersonal reading can escape operational and evidential correction.

Give the field measurable experiments without reducing every value to a metric. Distinguish empathy from inference, inspiration from commitment, and harmony from consent. The goal is a culture where meaning can become an artifact and where disagreement does not threaten belonging.

</section>

<section class="type-card">

## Stewards and operators — SJ field

An SJ-heavy field creates continuity: tested process, remembered detail, dependable support, and clear execution. Its strength is converting craft into an institution that survives individual heroics. Its risk is preserving a procedure after the environment or purpose has changed.

Introduce novelty through bounded pilots, migration plans, and explicit reasons. Make invisible maintenance and care work visible. Preserve a path for principled exceptions, then feed the result back into the standard. The goal is reliability that can learn.

</section>

<section class="type-card">

## Makers and troubleshooters — SP field

An SP-heavy field learns through direct contact: the current build, material behavior, visual judgment, fast diagnosis, and situational leverage. Its strength is making reality answer immediately. Its risk is optimizing the frame while the longer sequence, shared procedure, or downstream consequence remains invisible.

Bring this field into planning before abstractions harden, then add lightweight history and future-cost checks. Let the artifact speak, but require a handoff. The goal is responsive craft that leaves the system stronger after the immediate problem is solved.

</section>

# 18. The human engineering playbook

## The rendering review

Use this when a team is talking past itself. Each question intentionally recruits a different cognitive operation.

| Pass         | Ask                                        | Function pressure it counters |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------- |
| Capture      | What is demonstrably happening?            | Ni, Ne overreach              |
| History      | What has happened in comparable cases?     | Ne novelty bias               |
| Model        | What mechanism would explain it?           | Fe/Te vagueness               |
| Direction    | What future consequence matters?           | Se immediacy                  |
| Value        | What are we unwilling to trade away?       | Te-only optimization          |
| Coordination | Who is affected and what must be explicit? | Ti/Fi isolation               |
| Decision     | What will we try, measure, and revisit?    | Endless exploration           |

### A debugging protocol for social bugs

1. **Reproduce the issue.** Describe behavior, not motive.
2. **Separate render from model.** What happened? What story are we telling about why?
3. **Find the missing buffer.** Are evidence, history, alternatives, values, relationship, or execution absent?
4. **Choose a small test.** Prefer a reversible experiment over a total theory of the person.
5. **Review without blame.** A wrong model is information, not a moral failure.

## The cognitive budget

Every person has finite attention, working memory, energy, and tolerance for interruption. Good human engineering treats these as system constraints, not personality defects.

| Constraint          | Production symptom                       | Better design                            |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Context switching   | Shallow work, missed details, irritation | Batch questions and protect focus blocks |
| Ambiguous ownership | Passive waiting or competing work        | Name decision owner and input channels   |
| Salience overload   | Everything feels urgent                  | Rank by consequence and time horizon     |
| Hidden values       | Technically correct conflict             | State trade-offs and non-negotiables     |
| Missing feedback    | Repeated rework                          | Define observable acceptance signals     |

## Meetings as multi-pass rendering

A strong meeting does not ask every mind to perform every operation simultaneously. It sequences them.

```text
frame the problem -> inspect evidence -> generate options -> state constraints
      -> choose owner -> test -> report back -> revise
```

Start with Se and Si: show the artifact and history. Open Ne and Ni: generate and forecast. Use Ti and Te: define the mechanism and decision. End with Fi and Fe: state the value trade-off and the agreement between people. This is not typing the room. It is giving each operation a legitimate place.

## Development under pressure

Stress narrows the field. The systems builder may become privately certain. The explorer may multiply options. The operator may control. The harmonizer may avoid necessary conflict. The steward may cling to precedent. The maker may seek immediate action.

The correction is not shame. Name the pressure, reduce unnecessary urgency, restore sleep and information quality, and ask what the state has made invisible. Under sustained impairment, involve appropriate professional support rather than treating a team process as therapy.

# 19. Field exercises

## Exercise 1: map a render failure

Choose a recent visual or technical failure. Draw five boxes: evidence, interpretation, value, action, feedback. Fill each with one sentence. Mark the box where the project first went wrong. Then ask which function-pair check would have caught it.

## Exercise 2: translate a conflict

Take a sentence from a real disagreement: "You are overthinking it," "This is not practical," or "You do not understand the vision." Rewrite it as a valid request:

| Complaint                           | Hidden request                                              |
| ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| "You are overthinking it."          | Show a small test or decision boundary.                     |
| "This is not practical."            | State the constraint, owner, and implementation path.       |
| "You do not understand the vision." | Explain the future consequence and the value at stake.      |
| "Nobody listens."                   | Identify the affected people and create a feedback channel. |

## Exercise 3: design your counterweight

For one week, notice your default move under pressure. Do you act, analyze, imagine, forecast, preserve, optimize, protect values, or smooth the social field? Choose the paired correction and make it concrete. The goal is not balance as sameness. It is access to a perspective you otherwise exclude.

## Closing principle

The mature practitioner does not become typeless. They become less imprisoned by a preferred renderer. They can preserve the edge of their own perception while granting reality enough channels to correct them.
