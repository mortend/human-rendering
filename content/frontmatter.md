## Scope and safety note

This book is about collaboration, cognition, and engineering practice. It does not provide medical diagnosis, psychological treatment, employment assessment, or a scientific theory of neural function.

The cognitive functions are used as an interpretive vocabulary: eight recurring questions that can help a team inspect evidence, memory, possibility, trajectory, coherence, effectiveness, personal value, and shared relationship. A type pattern must never replace observation of an individual.

Do not use this material to make hiring, firing, promotion, clinical, or safety decisions. Do not use type language to excuse abuse, coercion, discrimination, severe impairment, or professional misconduct. When health, safety, reality testing, or functioning is at risk, qualified professional support takes priority over interpretation.

Technical examples are educational and schematic. Validate implementation details against the current documentation for the API, engine, hardware, and toolchain you use.

## How to use this book

The book has four reading paths:

1. **Conceptual path:** Chapters 1-12 explain attention, prediction, cognitive capture, symbolic discipline, and integration.
2. **Function path:** Chapters 13-15 provide the eight-function, complete interaction, and sixteen-type developmental atlases.
3. **Engineering path:** the Graphics Programming Laboratory and production scenarios translate the model into rendering work.
4. **Team path:** Chapters 15-19 address social dynamics, constellations, meetings, conflict, development, and exercises.
5. **Applied case path:** Chapter 20 follows the Meridian renderer rewrite from project contract through production failure, recovery, shipping, and retrospective.

The labels **KNOWN**, **MODEL**, and **SYMBOL** mark different levels of claim. Numbered citations point to the notes and sources. Code and diagrams are teaching sketches: they are designed to make relationships inspectable, not to prescribe an engine architecture.

Terminology is deliberately narrow. A **cognitive function** is one of the eight perspectives used in this book. A **function interaction** is a directed exchange in which one perspective offers information to another. A **type pattern** is a tentative developmental hypothesis about preference. A **type stack** refers only to the conventional ordering of dominant, auxiliary, tertiary, and inferior functions; it is not a container, identity, or temporary mood.
