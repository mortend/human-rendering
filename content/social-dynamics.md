# 16. Cognitive functions as social dynamics

Functions become social when one person's preferred signal changes another person's available choices. A private model becomes a plan; a value becomes a boundary; a remembered precedent becomes a rule; a felt atmosphere becomes a decision. The following pages describe recurring fields, not fixed roles.

<section class="social-card">

## Attention is power

Whatever a group repeatedly notices gains weight. Metrics, bug queues, reactions, review rituals, and dashboards are engineered salience systems. They can reveal reality, but they also decide which realities receive resources.

Se asks what is visible. Si asks what the system remembers. Ne asks what receives permission to become possible. Ni asks which future the current attention produces. Ti asks whether categories are coherent. Te asks what gets rewarded. Fi asks what disappears because it cannot be measured. Fe asks whose signal is admitted to the shared field.

Human engineering begins by auditing attention: what can interrupt the team, what cannot get on the agenda, and who pays to make an invisible problem visible?

</section>

<section class="social-card">

## Function currencies

Teams exchange more than labor. They exchange certainty, possibility, continuity, action, legitimacy, care, and meaning. A Te-heavy environment rewards visible completion. A Fe-heavy environment rewards coordination and social readability. Ti gains influence through explanatory precision; Fi through moral or aesthetic conviction; Se through direct effectiveness; Si through trusted memory; Ne through options; Ni through direction.

Conflict intensifies when one currency is treated as the only real value. The remedy is not equal time for every perspective. It is translation: show how continuity affects delivery, how values affect adoption, how social trust affects error reporting, and how present evidence changes the strategy.

</section>

<section class="social-card">

## Projection and the missing function

Groups often assign a neglected operation to one person. The visionary must always provide meaning. The operator must always rescue delivery. The caretaker must absorb tension. The skeptic must carry every objection. The rebel must create every alternative.

This looks efficient until the person becomes a caricature and the group loses access to the capacity itself. Projection is reduced when the team names the operation as shared work: everyone must sometimes inspect evidence, preserve history, generate alternatives, forecast, reason, execute, protect values, and coordinate.

</section>

<section class="social-card">

## Loops, grips, and team weather

In type language, a loop describes over-reliance on a preferred pattern; a grip describes destabilized behavior around a less comfortable process. These are interpretive terms, not clinical categories. At team scale, similar weather appears when the system repeats a narrow circuit.

A Ni-Te loop can become strategy without contact. Ne-Ti can become endless reframing. Si-Te can preserve procedure after purpose changes. Fe-Ni can turn shared meaning into certainty. Se-Ti can optimize the immediate problem while externalities accumulate.

Break a loop by adding the missing input, not by attacking the people identified with it.

</section>

<section class="social-card">

## Psychological safety and correction

A high-performing team needs a path for bad news. Psychological safety does not mean that every idea is accepted or every interaction is comfortable. It means that reporting an error, uncertainty, boundary, or dissent is not predictably punished through humiliation or exclusion. Systematic review evidence supports psychological safety as an important component of reliable team practice, while context and implementation still matter [7].

Ti needs permission to question the model. Fi needs permission to state a value conflict. Se needs permission to show the ugly capture. Si needs permission to mention the previous failure. Ne needs permission to propose an alternative. Ni needs permission to name a trajectory. Te needs permission to expose infeasibility. Fe needs permission to identify relational damage.

</section>

<section class="social-card">

## Manipulation as adversarial rendering

Manipulation changes another person's model while obstructing their ability to inspect the process. Common techniques increase urgency, narrow alternatives, fragment history, isolate feedback, reward compliance, or redefine disagreement as disloyalty.

The defense is procedural: slow reversible decisions, preserve records, compare accounts, maintain outside relationships, state consent, and separate consequences from threats. Type knowledge must never be used to identify a person's buttons for covert influence. Its ethical use is to make communication more legible while preserving agency.

</section>

<section class="social-card">

## Leadership as orchestration

Leadership is not one function. Vision without execution is theater; execution without value is machinery; harmony without truth is compliance; truth without communication is isolation. Leadership sequences operations and keeps correction possible.

A useful leader knows which mode the situation requires, makes the transition explicit, and prevents one mode from claiming permanent authority. During discovery, protect divergence. During emergency, clarify command. During review, restore dissent. During integration, connect results to purpose and people.

</section>

<section class="social-card">

## Conflict as incompatible render settings

Many conflicts begin with different thresholds rather than opposite goals. One person wants more evidence; another sees delay. One wants more options; another sees instability. One protects values; another sees obstruction. One protects harmony; another sees avoidance.

Translate the position into a threshold: How much evidence is enough? When does exploration close? Which value is non-negotiable? What level of disagreement can the relationship hold? Once thresholds are visible, the team can design a test instead of diagnosing a character.

</section>

<section class="social-card">

## Groups as attractor landscapes

A group develops states it falls into easily: crisis mode, brainstorming, deference, blame, humor, craft, control, or silence. Repetition deepens the attractor. New members learn the path through consequences rather than policy.

Change requires more than a declared value. Alter the cue, reward, transition, and feedback. If every late issue produces heroic rescue, the system learns lateness and heroics. If early uncertainty receives curiosity and help, the system learns disclosure.

</section>

<section class="social-card">

## Integration is recoverable plurality

An integrated group is not one that reaches constant consensus. It can move among modes without splitting into permanent camps. Evidence can correct vision without humiliating the visionary. Values can constrain efficiency without shaming the operator. A boundary can enter without ending belonging.

The practical sign of health is recoverability: after urgency, can reflection return? After conflict, can cooperation return? After failure, can curiosity return? After certainty, can revision return?

</section>
