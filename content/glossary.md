# Glossary

**Active inference** — a family of models in which perception and action participate in reducing uncertainty or prediction error. Used here only as scientific context, not as a synonym for MBTI.

**Agency** — the capacity to choose, refuse, revise, and act. Any ethical use of type language must preserve it.

**Attractor** — a state or pattern a dynamic system tends to enter or remain in. In this book, a model for recurring individual or group states.

**Auxiliary function** — the second process in a conventional type stack; commonly interpreted as a counterweight and support for the dominant.

**Capture** — the condition in which one signal, state, interpretation, or operation becomes difficult to leave.

**Cognitive budget** — finite attention, working memory, energy, and switching capacity available in a situation.

**Cognitive function** — in this book, one of eight Jungian/MBTI-derived perspectives expressed as a recurring question. It is not treated as a neural module.

**Constraint** — a boundary that gives work form: time, memory, hardware, responsibility, values, or safety.

**Counterbalance** — an excluded perspective deliberately recruited to correct overuse of a preferred process.

**Dominant function** — the process conventionally understood to organize a type pattern's default orientation.

**Fe** — Extraverted Feeling: What matters between us? Shared values, relationship, atmosphere, and coordination.

**Fi** — Introverted Feeling: What matters to me? Personal value, integrity, authenticity, and conscience.

**Field** — the social arrangement of attention, incentives, authority, relationships, and available actions around a group.

**Function currency** — the kind of value through which a function tends to gain influence, such as clarity, continuity, possibility, throughput, or legitimacy.

**Grip** — type-language shorthand for destabilized behavior associated with a less comfortable process. Not a clinical category.

**Inferior function** — the fourth process in a conventional stack; often described as both vulnerable and developmentally important.

**Integration** — increased access to differentiated perspectives without erasing preference; visible as improved correction and movement.

**KNOWN** — a claim presented as established or reasonably supported by evidence.

**Loop** — repeated use of a narrow cognitive circuit that excludes a needed counter-perspective. An interpretive model, not a diagnosis.

**MBTI physics** — this book's engineering metaphor for interactions among cognitive perspectives, social forces, constraints, and trajectories.

**Meta-awareness** — awareness of a thought, feeling, or state as an event rather than total identification with it.

**MODEL** — a useful explanatory bridge that should remain open to correction.

**Ne** — Extraverted Intuition: What else could this become? Possibility, association, alternatives, and reframing.

**Ni** — Introverted Intuition: Where is this leading? Compression, trajectory, pattern, and direction.

**Openness** — capacity for novelty, possibility, association, learning, and revision.

**Projection** — attributing disowned or insufficiently recognized material to another person; used cautiously here for role assignment and shadow dynamics.

**Psychological safety** — a team condition in which interpersonal risk, error reporting, uncertainty, or dissent can enter without predictable humiliation or exclusion.

**Reality testing** — comparing an interpretation with evidence, alternatives, records, consequences, and independent perspectives.

**Recoverable movement** — the capacity to enter a state and later shift when context or feedback changes; the book's practical definition of flexibility.

**Salience** — priority or significance assigned to a signal through goals, learning, emotion, novelty, reward, and context.

**Se** — Extraverted Sensing: What is here now? Immediate evidence, direct contact, and responsive action.

**Shadow** — aspects of a person or group that are excluded, disowned, or insufficiently integrated; also the failure potential of a strength.

**Si** — Introverted Sensing: What does this resemble? Memory, continuity, comparison, precedent, and learned detail.

**Stack** — shorthand for **type stack**: the conventional ordering of dominant, auxiliary, tertiary, and inferior cognitive functions in one MBTI type pattern. The term describes an ordering, not a person or temporary state.

**SYMBOL** — a metaphorical, archetypal, geometric, religious, or cultural correspondence; meaningful without being a causal mechanism.

**Te** — Extraverted Thinking: What works in the shared world? Measurement, organization, execution, and external criteria.

**Tertiary function** — the third process in a conventional stack; often experienced as familiar but less mature or less stabilizing than the first two.

**Ti** — Introverted Thinking: Does this make sense internally? Distinction, mechanism, consistency, and conceptual precision.

**Translation** — converting a perspective's signal into terms another perspective can evaluate without erasing its original value.

**Type pattern** — a tentative hypothesis about preference and development, not a complete identity or prediction of ability. This is the book's preferred term for discussing a type as a whole.
